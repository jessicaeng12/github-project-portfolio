IPA Drying Station (2025)

CAD design and fabrication of a drying station that increased the effectiveness and efficiency of SLA 3D printing processes.

Project Overview

SLA printed parts require an IPA rinse followed by drying before final curing. The existing workflow lacked a dedicated solution for this important drying step. This project involved the design and fabrication of an IPA drying station that was capable of circulating air efficiently, holding multiple resin prints, and fitting within the company’s size constraints. My team of three interns developed this station over a three-month internship, with a focus on understanding how larger-scale engineering projects are executed in an industry setting.

The IPA drying station successfully met company requirements by increasing the effectiveness and efficiency of SLA 3D printing processes. The project demonstrated effective translation from CAD design to physical fabrication while accounting for real-world manufacturing constraints. This experience strengthened my skills in mechanical design, design for manufacturability, and hands-on machining, and reinforced the importance of cross-functional collaboration in an industry setting.

My Role

  • Led the CAD design in SolidWorks, translating initial concepts into a manufacturable design
  • Verified all critical dimensions and clearances to ensure proper fits during assembly
  • Created detailed engineering drawings, including part drawings and exploded assembly drawings
  • Used mentor feedback to iterate on the design and finalize a manufacturable solution
  • Played a key role in design discussions focused on efficiency, airflow, assembly, strategy, and material selection based on available equipment and resources
  • Assisted in the manufacturing and assembly process, gaining hands-on experience with machining and fabrication techniques

EMG Driven Exoskeleton: Trigger Finger Rehabilitation (2025)

Electromyography (EMG) driven exoskeleton for the rehabilitation of trigger finger through bicep and tricep contractions

Project Overview

For my rehabilitation robotics course, we were tasked with developing an EMG-controlled exoskeleton designed to provide controlled and repeatable joint extension. Our design focused on the application of exoskeletons for the rehabilitation of trigger finger, focusing on the metacarpophalangeal (MCP) joint. By enabling user-driven control, the device promotes active neuromuscular engagement while offering a cost-effective and adaptable alternative to traditional trigger finger therapy.

The EMG-controlled finger exoskeleton achieved smooth, repeatable actuation without interference from antagonist coactivation. Under EMG control, biceps and triceps activation enabled intuitive, bidirectional finger movement, demonstrating the system potential as an accessible and adaptable rehabilitation tool for trigger finger therapy. Future improvements to the device include increasing sensitivity to allow EMG control from the forearm and improving usability for patients with limited strength. Reducing excess wiring and material along the arm would enhance comfort, safety, and practicality for daily use.

My Role

  • Acquired and analyzed MYOware EMG signals from the biceps and triceps, including baseline characterization and signal validation
  • Implemented threshold-based EMG detection logic to differentiate resting and active muscle states
  • Programmed an interval-based sinusoidal motor control algorithm to drive smooth, repeatable extension and flexion of the linear servo motor
  • Utilized a joystick input to simulate EMG activation thresholds during development, enabling rapid tuning and debugging before full EMG integration
  • Finalized the embedded control software (Arduino/C++) by integrating EMG signal processing and motor control into a unified control loop
  • Performed kinematic calculations relating actuator linear displacement to MCP joint rotation, enabling comparison of desired vs. measured joint motion

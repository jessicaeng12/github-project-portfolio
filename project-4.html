Pulley System: Trigger Finger Rehabilitation (2024)

Electromechanical rehabilitation device to assist with finger flexion and grip recovery from tigger finger using a pulley system.

Project Overview

Trigger finger can lead to pain, limited finger mobility, and reduced grip strength, particularly when conservative treatments fail, and surgical intervention is required. Inspired by a real-world rehabilitation challenge following trigger finger surgery, this sophomore design project focused on developing a mechanical rehabilitation device to assist with finger flexion and grip recovery. The design utilized a pulley-driven system with multiple operating modes to close the hand using strings routed around a central spool. The project emphasized mechanical simplicity, adaptability to individual fingers, and hands-on design experience using SolidWorks CAD and Arduino-based control concepts.

The pulley-based rehabilitation device demonstrated a feasible mechanical approach to assisting finger flexion and grip recovery following trigger finger rehabilitation. This project strengthened my skills in concept-driven design, CAD modeling, and hands-on assembly, while highlighting the importance of user-centered design informed by real rehabilitation needs.

My Role

  • Led concept development, generating and evaluating multiple rehabilitation device concepts
  • Designed custom finger attachments in Solidworks, individually sized for each finger to improve fit and usability
  • Played a key role in narrowing the design to a pulley-based actuation system with multi-mode hand closure
  • Created the full assembly model and exploded drawing used to visualize and build the device
  • Assisted with the physical assembly, including part collection and final device integration
